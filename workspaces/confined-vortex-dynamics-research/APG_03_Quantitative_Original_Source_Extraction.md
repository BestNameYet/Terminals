## Evidence definitions

- **Reported measurement (E):** apparatus observation or value stated in the source. Its error bounds and regime remain source-specific.
- **Source model / derived parameter (T):** quantity calculated by the original author using stated equations or assumptions (e.g. ideal-jet speed from differential pressure).
- **Source interpretation (I):** author-proposed physical mechanism, retained as conditional and tested against alternatives.
- **Analyst inference (H):** controlled physics hypothesis for transfer to other devices; not a measured finding.
- **Unavailable (U):** no verified original measured value in the inspected text. Unknown must not become zero.

Coordinates: in the diffuser, x grows toward the outlet, and p_e(x) is near-wall external/static pressure; **dp_e/dx > 0** is APG. In the cylindrical captive vortex use r outward and z along chamber axis, tangential azimuth about z. In a feedback oscillator the main jet axis and left/right attachment-wall normals are separate from the feedback-channel coordinate. In a jet-driven cavity the inlet axial jet and outlet axial path do not imply a tangential swirling vortex. A single pressure loss between supply and outlet is NOT a measurement of the local wall APG.

## R01 — Elliott G. Reid (1953), NACA Technical Note 2888

**Original:** *Performance Characteristics of Plane-Wall Two-Dimensional Diffusers*, NASA/NACA original full PDF: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930083792/downloads/19930083792.pdf (1953 original publication; scan/index later). **Source:** title, Summary, apparatus, parameters, Results and Accuracy pp. 22 ff, Tables II–III and Figures (not all manually digitized). Evidence **E**.

**Construction/state:** plane-wall nearly two-dimensional divergent passages with identical entrance sections, varying included divergence and length/area ratio; downstream discharge directly into a large plenum. Source includes **22 plain diffusers** as well as asymmetric, extended and centrally partitioned modifications. Air at a nominally maintained **entrance dynamic pressure q1 = 60 lbf/ft²**; source describes a thin, fully turbulent entering boundary layer. Measured wall/inlet/exit static pressures; several rake exit surveys per configuration. Different included wall angles, exit extensions and central partition are **separate configuration families**, not evidence of one fixed geometry.

**Original parameter definitions (source pp. 15–22):** entrance-to-exit static pressure recovery coefficient **C_PR = (p2 − p1)/q1**, area ratio **R = A2/A1** (for their two-dimensional case R = W2/W1); the **ideal inviscid** reference C_PR,i = 1 − 1/R²; source pressure efficiency eta_p = C_PR/C_PR,i. This `R` is a diffuser area ratio, NOT Reynolds number. Requiring both pressure stations and the outlet plenum state matters; an apparent increase in static pressure is not equal to net total-pressure gain.

**Measured/observed:** source describes steady, sometimes **asymmetric but *unseparated*** flow, as well as a sharp deterioration in steadiness after the angle corresponding to maximum static pressure recovery. Auxiliary experiments identified **streamwise pressure gradient rather than included angle alone** as governing separation in these layouts. A thin central longitudinal partition suppressed separation in some short diffusers; an exit extension raised pressure recovery. These are controlled source-specific observations, not general diffuser optimization ratios.

**Uncertainty:** Reid estimates for less-than-optimum divergence static-pressure recording errors **≤ ±0.01 q1**; for the unsteady over-diverged models, some pressure measurements might differ from true temporal means by as much as **0.03 q1**. The latter is an **uncertain mean-pressure error**, not a standard deviation. Source limits include quasi-two-dimensional assumption, velocity distortion, plenum coupling, apparatus Reynolds dependence, and difficult unsteady averages. No universal onset half-angle or single critical pressure gradient is imported. Figures/Tables II–III require separate machine-checked digitization for a pointwise pressure recovery dataset.

## F09 — Löffler, Ebert & Weiss (2021), *Fluids* 6(4):166

**Original:** https://www.mdpi.com/2311-5521/6/4/166 ; DOI 10.3390/fluids6040166. **Source:** original full HTML Sections 3–5; Tables 1–2; equations 1–11; Figures 3, 6, 9, 11–20. Evidence **E + N-V + source I**.

**Construction/state:** compressed-air plenum feeds a **single bistable power-jet amplifier**, split into two attachment/settling chambers, a splitter and **two actual external feedback loops** returning flow/pressure to control ports; outlet nozzle ejects pulses to an **external aerodynamic boundary layer**. The device is not merely a Coanda jet lacking return passages. Primary/secondary attachment walls can cause **distinct fast and slow switching states**. Ref variant power-jet area **15.12 mm²**, maximum settling-chamber area **53.67 mm²**, nominal outlet nozzle area **30.24 mm²**, nominal feedback partition **50/50**. The FR variant changes feedback partition **80/20**, with the same power jet and outlet areas; the Z variant increases settling chamber area to **96.27 mm²**; S variant power nozzle **6.72 mm²**, settling chamber **23.85 mm²**, outlet held **30.24 mm²** (Table 1). Actual outlet open area changes with a moving throttle device.

**Source definitions:** throttle ratio **T_OP = A_PJ/A_O**, where A_O is the *unblocked area of one outlet*, not the tabulated nominal total; equivalent power jet diameter D_PJ = 2 sqrt(A_PJ/pi). Feedback ratios L+ = L_FL/D_PJ and D+ = D_FL/D_PJ. Source's internal Strouhal **St_FO = f_pulse L_FL / v_jet** is **NOT** the reduced frequency of the separately controlled outer surface (F+ = f_e l_te/U_inf).

**Measured numbers:** absolute mass-flow instrument accuracy **±1.6%**; repeated measurement **±2sigma mass flow = 0.048 g/s** and **±2sigma frequency = 1.6 Hz**. Original feedback/settling pressure probes recorded at **8,192 samples/s**; combined static pressure error **<22 Pa**, and source's derived outlet-velocity maximum error **0.35% (1.3 m/s)**. Pressure tap topology: four plenum ports combined on one sensor, plus feedback loop, power nozzle and settling-chamber taps; nonplenum sensor tubes **25 mm**; symmetric auxiliary ports blocked. **Source reports experimental validation values 2,360 Hz at 2 g/s and 545 Hz at 4 g/s**, for the reference case (Section 3.3/Fig. 9). Increasing-flow sweep initiates near **0.8 g/s and 2,198 Hz**, jumps to an approximately **500 Hz** low-frequency mode above around **2.8–3 g/s** in the particular setup (Section 4/Fig. 11); authors also report **hysteresis**, but did not map its full boundary.

**Source model/phase evidence:** 3D compressible URANS with Spalart–Allmaras wall modeling, inlet **0.002 kg/s at 293 K** and outlet **101,325 Pa** is *not* a direct velocity-field measurement. For a slower modeled state, authors report **5 g/s; 613 Hz** (Fig. 12). For feedback length **2.1 m (L+≈520)** versus **4.1 m (L+≈980)** at given configuration, source finds frequency roughly halves; their **166 Hz vs 85 Hz** comparison is **an estimated acoustic-time-scale** using a = 350 m/s, not those exact measured values. Authors identify leading/trailing feedback pressure waves and the secondary-wall attachment distinction, including an approximate control-port pair of **1.6×10^5 vs 1.3×10^5 Pa** in one low-frequency CFD phase and **1.1×10^5 vs 1.3×10^5 Pa** in high-frequency phase (Fig. 20); these are **phase-specific modeled port pressures**, not a streamwise outer-surface APG measurement.

**Causality/limits:** preserve outlet throttling, feedback length/diameter, wall attachment and pressure load simultaneously; never ascribe the low/high transition solely to vorticity or vortex breakdown. The external APG being controlled is **not shown to be the same internal gradient** as within the oscillator. The authors' fit curves St_FO,1≈M_jet^-0.9, St_FO,2≈3M_jet^-1 and St_FO,3≈5M_jet^-1.1 are *empirical for that topology*, not thresholds transferable to other devices or a named vortex mode.

## F05 — Václav Tesař (2019), *Energies* 12:3341

**Original:** *Delay by Spin-Up of Captive Vortex*, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/en12173341 ; user-supplied original full PDF `cfd_oscillators_refset.zip!/energies-12-03341-v2.pdf`. **Source:** section 6.3–6.4, workshop geometry Fig. 11, measured FFT Fig. 12, frequency-vs-flow Fig. 13, invariant Figs. 14–16, Eqs. 1–6. Evidence **E + T + I**.

**Construction/state:** a monostable jet deflector drives a **single passive cylindrical vortex pocket** through a tangential pathway; a **radial easy-flow inlet** competes with the tangential flow. Central outlet through a bottom cover, wall torque, and jet capture govern delay. Selected case **B of Fig. 9**, developed by successive cut-and-try cavity changes rather than simply scaling one geometry. Vortex pocket **D = 52.3 mm**; thin laser-cut PMMA cavity plate **h = 2 mm** sealed between thick top/bottom plates by **eight screws**. Source describes a small wall cut-out **r = 3.26 mm** with position **26.03 mm** (workshop Fig. 11). These are real seal/channel features, unlike a generic unsealed 'vortex island'.

**Measurements:** dry air supply from compressed source measured with Omega FMA1827 mass meter up to nominal 50 g/min, output with Dantec CTA 54T30 hot-wire probe, FFT sample rate **8 kHz**, **262,144 samples** per supply setting. Fundamental switching frequencies span **11.7–45.26 Hz**, with low-flow departure from linear trend at **about 0.3 g/s**. Fig. 12 includes an example supply mass flow **0.374 g/s**; exact fundamental peak for that trace is left undigitized. The model shows clear harmonics; a single spectral line alone would not establish vortex causation in an unrelated apparatus.

**Original theory:** Strouhal Sh = f1 b / w (supply width b, supply nozzle bulk velocity w), and vortex contents Mc = pi D² h/(4v), where v is the **specific volume**, not velocity. Proposed delay parameter **Te = pi f1 D² h /(4 v dot(m))**, with empirical mean **Te = 0.596 ± 0.033** from **30 points in two repeated tests** (source Eq. 6). The quoted ±0.033 is reported as a **standard deviation**, not sensor uncertainty or a 95% prediction interval. This compact delay fit is a **source-derived correlation for its captured-vortex pocket**; it does not prove every observed oscillator is captive-vortex-driven.

**Controls needed:** block the vortex inlet while bounding load change; compare direct radial path, tangential path, cavity volume and outlet. Holding total supply pressure alone does not hold the flow split/torque fixed.

## F06 — Abdrashitov, Marfin & Plakhova (2021), AIP Conf. Proc. 2351:040059

**Original:** DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0052206 ; supplied complete 6-page PDF `cfd_oscillators_refset.zip!/experimental-study-of-the-jet-driven-helmholtz-oscillator-54o29xtt79.pdf`. **Source:** original Fig. 1 section, apparatus and results pp. 2–5, Figs. 3–4. Evidence **E + T + source I**.

**Construction/state:** cylindrical plastic resonator chamber with **front sharp-edged axial inlet nozzle** and **rear sharp-edged coaxial outlet**, each through a cover. An internal microphone and pressure fitting are on the inlet cover. Flow enters from room air, drawn by a **vacuum reservoir ~10^5 times larger** than the resonator chamber; acoustic isolation is a stated *source assumption*. Inlet nozzle diameter d1, inlet length l1, chamber diameter D and axial length L, exit diameter d2 and exit length l2 are separate geometry factors. The exit length is fixed at **10 mm**; published example geometry **d1 = 12 mm, l1 = 25 mm; d2 = 16 mm, l2 = 10 mm, L = 18 mm** (Fig. 4 caption). Neither port is tangential, so do not assert an azimuthal core.

**Measurements and source definitions:** slow differential pressure across the nozzle yields assumed ideal jet speed **W² = 2ΔP/rho**, where entrance loss coefficient is not part of this estimate. The source explicitly defines **Sh_L = f L/W**, **Re_L = L W/nu**: length L is the cavity/jet length and f the **generation** frequency, distinct from f1 in Tesař. Source reports acoustic frequency measurement error **about 0.4%**. Microphone detects chamber pressure oscillations; a pressure transducer detects slow nozzle pressure difference. With gradual speed increase, reported jet-tone starts near **W≈2 m/s**, at tens of hertz, below the chamber natural resonance; higher-speed modes can occur when forcing aligns with cavity response. Source distinguishes **jet-tone frequency** from the chamber's independently measured zero-flow natural frequency, and observed regimes where modes disappear or are replaced as speed increases.

**Geometry-dependent results:** for short nozzle **l1/d1≈0.3**, source reports strongest amplitude with short chamber **L/d1≈0.5** and declining amplitude as L grows. Nozzle lengths **9 mm and 16.5 mm** give l1/d1=0.75 and 1.375 for d1=12 mm, with different amplitude; at inlet length about **2d1** a source-reported optimum appears near **L≈1.5d1**; rounding the inlet lip changes amplitude. Exact per-curve amplitudes should be read from graph axes with controlled digitization; do not invent them.

**Interpretation discipline:** authors describe jet-tone and cavity acoustic mode interactions; these are original source interpretations under well-defined two-port/reservoir acoustics, not proof that any unrelated audible tone equals a named Helmholtz resonance. Axial jet separation/reattachment, outlet-edge forcing and cavity compliance remain alternatives until phase/baseline controls discriminate them.

## F02 — Václav Tesař (2015), *Acta Mechanica et Automatica* 9(4)

**Original:** *Fluidic Generator of Microbubbles — Oscillator with Gas Flow Reversal for a Part of Period*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1515/ama-2015-0032 ; supplied complete nine-page PDF `cfd_oscillators_refset.zip!/Fluidic_Generator_Of_Microbubbles_-_Oscillator_Wit.pdf`. **Source:** Sections 4–6; measured bubble images Figs. 5–7; diagrams Figs. 8–12; original Eqs. 8–9. Evidence **E (demonstration) + T (proposed accumulator design) + I (mechanistic)**.

**Geometry/states:** air flows into an attachment-wall **monostable diverter**, then an axial jet-pump/divider whose outlets connect separately to (a) a submerged water aerator and (b) an air accumulator expressed with a **U-tube water column**. A vent connects the diverter to atmosphere. Air supply/outlet paths are pressure/load-dependent; submerged depth and aerator channel resistance are part of the hydrodynamic system. During charging, air flows to the aerator and accumulator. Increased hydraulic load switches the jet into the vent, and stored potential energy drives a temporary **reverse gas flow** through aerator passages, preventing neighboring newly formed bubbles from merging. The paper includes a more elaborate double-acting design; the **U-tube circuit figures are proposed design architecture**, not a full independently instrumented test of every drawn variant.

**Reported experimental observation:** an aerator passage/initial bubble size **about 0.6 mm** is shown in source Fig. 5/6; image tracking supports lack of near-exit bubble conjunction under the successful temporary-flow-reversal setup. The source explicitly reports failures when larger amplifiers, longer/tortuous aerator passages or greater submergence increased hydraulic resistance. Do **not** infer a universal measured bubble-size distribution or verified nano-/microbubble performance under other pressures.

**Original model:** using its U-tube notation, source writes **ΔP = g ΔH** in terms of the selected head variable (its pressure/head notation should be verified dimensionally before numerical application), and an approximate liquid-column frequency **f=(1/2pi)sqrt(g/l)**, where l is the source-defined liquid-column length. Check the actual U-tube geometry and mass/inertance before using this idealization; the present paper does not supply a complete uncertainty budget for the particular 0.6 mm observation. PMMA flow cavities for one photographed double-acting model were laser-cut into **3 mm plates** (Fig. 12); do not assign this to every schematic variant.

**Distinct limiting state:** oscillator switching and useful *reverse* aerator flow are not equivalent. A high hydraulic load may allow switch-like operation yet eliminate reverse pumping, changing bubble coalescence. This is a strong counterexample to assuming an oscillator automatically provides the intended interfacial outcome.

| Diffuser APG separation | R01 upstream-to-downstream static pressure, near-wall reversal / recovery, geometry-specific critical regime | Changes in exit plenum or inlet layer cause asymmetric pressure recovery | Hold inlet q1 and wall geometry fixed; change exit length/central partition; sample wall pressures and near-wall velocities |

## Source-independence and quantitative limits

- **Five full original texts** were read for these selected values, with article figures and tables inspected where accessible. A bounded extraction is **not** full raw-series digitization, and multiple versions/configurations of one article do not count as independent publications.
- The **F02/F05 Tesař papers are one investigator lineage**, though their physical circuits differ. The Löffler F09 paper and referenced external wind-tunnel control work are not a proof of quantitative external APG recovery in the particular actuator study. Reid's 1953 diffuser includes true APG tests, but is not a vortex chamber.
- **F17 Bergadà et al.** DOI 10.3390/s21175768 was readable at abstract/metadata level (compressible DNS, nominal Re 12,410 and 18,617). Its full text was **not reliably accessible** during this source processing pass. Its detailed pressure-wave mechanism, feedback lengths, and onset thresholds remain **unverified here**, and it is not scored as extracted.
- Full pointwise Tables II/III from the Reid scan, the Tesař F05 frequency curves, the Abdrashitov F06 amplitudes, and some F09 PIV/CFD field figures remain **non-digitized**. These are listed as future source-figure tasks; lack of digitization is not treated as zero or a negative result.
- Acoustic device-state ledger: **no project recordings inspected**. No transfer of F06 named mode or F05 frequency range to any project apparatus. Geometry, microphone chain, outlet volume, inlet blockage, flow strength, time window and exact baseline are prerequisites.
