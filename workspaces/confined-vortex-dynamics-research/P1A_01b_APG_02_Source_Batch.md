### Engineering-physics distinctions used for source coding
**Coordinate and pressure definition.** For a named near-wall streamwise direction s, a mean adverse external pressure gradient is dp_e/ds > 0. An oscillator's static pressure distribution, supply-to-exit pressure loss, feedback-channel pressure-wave gradient and a swirl chamber's positive radial pressure gradient are different quantities. The data must name pressure-tap positions, time/phase, wall orientation and source forcing. In unsteady and 3D flow, reversal of wall shear does not on its own prove a separated rotational layer; Simpson 1989 is a counterexample-focused review.

**Six topology classes.** (1) Externally imposed APG diffusers and partially stalled internal ducts: F13–F15. (2) Airfoil/wing separation controlled by periodic forcing *external to the actuator*: F12 and F21; the first may include oscillatory blowing, the second synthetic jets, and neither establishes an internal oscillator APG. (3) Wall-attachment/feedback-channel oscillators: F10, F16–F19, F22–F25. (4) Historic pneumatic oscillators and RC/relaxation logic: F26–F29; pressure capacitance and feedback conductance must not be conflated with physical free-jet self-switching. (5) Free-jet actuator measurements: F11, distinguished from external APG tests. (6) Cavity/acoustic suppression: F20, with a separate controlled cavity; a named Helmholtz resonance is a diagnosis requiring geometry and phase evidence.

**Feedback versus local instability.** The source-specific claims in Bobusch 2013 and Woszidlo 2015 associate switching with growth of a recirculation/separation bubble fed by actual connected feedback channels. The Nili 2020 *numerical* design uses two chambers, a mid-throat and physical feedback paths; it reports no oscillation without those feedback paths in its modeled configuration. Bergadà et al. 2021 numerically reported traveling pressure waves and that feedback-channel pressure, rather than momentum flux alone, dominated their studied compressible configurations. These findings need not be true for a feedback-free oscillator or a non-similar cavity. Seo et al. 2018 model an attachment-to-outlet length scale, while Chen et al. 2025 examine changing chamber effective length; preserve each study's exact geometric definition before comparing Strouhal numbers.

**Pressure loss and load.** Partial-stall diffusers and active fluidic oscillators require distinct pressure budgets. Report supply pressure, upstream station, chamber static taps, feedback-channel taps and downstream loading. Pressure loss is not evidence of APG location, vortex breakdown or the stored acoustic energy in a compliant outlet volume. For all oscillators record characteristic hydraulic diameter, actual fluid path lengths, Reynolds definition, feedback loop cross section, operating-pressure/flow source curve, measured oscillation state and alternative nonoscillating states.

**Patent verification tier.** F26 Plessey/Davis (US3604443A; earliest indexed priority 1968-11-28) describes a fluidic resistor-capacitor and bistable/amplifier topology; F27 Warren (US3016066A; 1960-01-22), F28 Horton/Bowles (US3185166A; 1960-04-08), F29 Spyropoulos (US3217727A; 1963-09-10) are three distinct source family leads. F28 has corresponding GB985700A metadata; never double count it as a second independent invention. Publication years differ from priority years. Claims and device figures are discoverable on linked patent pages, but no full figure-by-figure claim analysis or operating-performance measurement is asserted here. Do not infer the historical title of first-ever oscillator patent without wider family-priority comparison (including Zalmanzon).

### Minimal discriminating test matrix
| Hypothesis | Strong observation | Specific counter-test / control |
|---|---|---|
| External adverse-gradient separation | Wall/edge streamwise pressure increase accompanied by near-wall layer departure and velocity topology | Keep oscillator supply fixed and vary external APG; locate attachment/reattachment |
| Fluidic feedback-path switching | Phase-linked growth of bubble and pressure/flow response in actual feedback passage | Block one path while documenting new loss and flow allocation; change loop length alone |
| Pressure-wave dominated feedback | Phase-resolved feedback pressure force exceeds flow-momentum disturbance within stated model | Add pressure taps and compare channel pressure phase with injected momentum |
| Internal vortex-driven switching | Three-dimensional time-resolved vorticity cores and correlated jet path | Enforce symmetry, remove/alter feedback, change vortex pocket; compare stationary alternate states |
| Relaxation/compliance oscillator | Charge/discharge pressure history linked to cavity capacity and restriction | Vary compliant volume and fluidic resistance separately |
| Acoustic cavity coupling | Oscillation and phase shift with cavity length/volume and acoustic impedance | Change cavity volume or neck length keeping jet supply unchanged; matched baseline |
| Core swirl breakdown | Axial zero/reversal in swirling core, not merely a separated wall layer | Reduce signed swirl at fixed source pressure/exit load; image 3D axial velocity |
| Sensor/recorder artifact | Peaks unchanged by flow-state controls or microphone position | Ambient baseline, microphone movement, tubing impedance, sampling-chain audit |
